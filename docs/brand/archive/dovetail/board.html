The swipe: bump it.

In a kitchen you bump a ticket when it is dealt with. Bind's swipe is the same gesture: slide a ticket sideways off the rail. Right bumps it, left holds it.

Drag a ticket, press a button, or focus a ticket and use the arrow keys.

What it does, and what it must not

Swipe is for acknowledging. Delivery notes, feedback summaries, "seen" items. Anything that spends money or promises something to a customer is a tap and a confirm, never a swipe: one slip of a thumb must not buy butter.

Thresholds. Past 35% of the ticket's width, or a quick flick, it commits. Short of that, it seats back with a small knock. The ticket follows the finger 1 to 1.

Always a button too. Bump and Hold are real buttons, and the arrow keys work on a focused ticket. The result is announced ("Flour arrived: bumped").

Honest state. The ticket leaves only after the request succeeds. If it fails it comes back with "Needs you" and a reason. This demo has no server, so it only simulates that.

On it

Loading: tick, tock.

The yolk piece passes the seat twice per cycle and pauses on each pass, like a drawer being worked. It never shows a made-up percentage. With reduced motion it holds still, 5 units off the seat.

The loading screen shows what is actually being done ("Opening your workspace"). In the product those lines come from the server. Here they are simulated.

The mark: a dovetail.

Two pieces joined so that neither can pull away sideways, only slide along the seam. Offset means "in motion". Seated means "done". The yolk piece is whatever is in hand right now.

Colour

Yolk is the only loud colour and it means "in hand". Finished work goes quiet. Red is for needs-you and nothing else.
