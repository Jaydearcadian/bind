# Bind Brand Direction and Creative Brief

**Role:** Chief Brand Designer, Creative Director, UX Strategist, and Visual Systems Partner  
**Product:** Bind  
**Context:** Tameion Agents Hackathon  
**Purpose:** Establish one coherent brand, marketing experience, in-app experience, and demo story that can be implemented quickly without misrepresenting the product.

This brief applies the Chief Brand Designer framework to Bind. It is deliberately opinionated. It keeps the supplied Superpower screenshot as a reference for editorial pacing, photography, spaciousness, strong type, and confident calls to action, but does not copy that brand's identity, copy, exact layouts, assets, or health-specific material.

---

## A. Brand Core

### What Bind is

Bind is a business-side agent deployment and coordination layer. A business defines its context, operating rules, permissions, supplier preferences, and spending authority. Agents can then handle repeatable operational work and coordinate a workflow, while important decisions remain governed by the business's rules.

For the Tameion slice, the concrete story is a kitchen or small food business handling low stock. Bind connects the stock signal, supplier request, policy decision, approval gate, Arc Testnet settlement evidence where applicable, and the still-open physical fulfilment record.

Bind is **not** primarily a chatbot, no-code agent builder, agent marketplace, crypto wallet, or payment/clearing engine. Payments are one controlled part of a business workflow, not the whole proposition.

### One-line positioning

**Bind coordinates AI agents around the way a business actually works, with clear limits and a record of every important action.**

### Brand promise

**More routine work handled. Control stays with the business.**

### Emotional hook

The business owner wants relief from repetitive coordination, not another system to supervise and not a system that can spend, promise, or decide beyond its authority. Bind should feel like getting operational help without losing the keys.

### Target audiences

**Primary for the demo:** owner-operators of small, process-heavy businesses such as a kitchen, café, caterer, or local service business.

**Secondary:** operations managers who coordinate stock, suppliers, invoices, fulfilment, and exceptions; technical evaluators assessing whether agents operate under enforceable rules rather than prompts alone.

**Longer-term audience:** service providers and external agents that need a controlled, machine-readable way to request business services. Do not build this broader interface as a fake feature in the first slice.

### Brand personality

- **Grounded:** speak in real business tasks, not abstract “agentic transformation.”
- **Capable:** confident without hype or oversized promises.
- **Warm:** human and approachable, not sterile enterprise software.
- **Composed:** clear status, quiet UI, no alarmist decoration.
- **Accountable:** show what was decided, under which authority, and what remains unfinished.
- **Practical:** every main page answers “what happened?” and “what happens next?”

### Brand archetype

**The Operator + The Steward + The Connector.**

- The Operator knows the workflow and keeps work moving.
- The Steward respects permission boundaries and protects the owner's authority.
- The Connector coordinates people, agents, money, suppliers, and fulfilment without claiming they are the same thing.

### What makes Bind memorable

The memorable idea is not “AI agents for business.” It is that three things must stay connected for agents to be useful in the real economy:

1. **Context:** the business's products, supplies, timelines, preferences, and policies.
2. **Authority:** what an agent can do, spend, or approve, and where a human must decide.
3. **Fulfilment:** what happened outside the software, separate from payment or on-chain settlement.

This “threefold bind” becomes a subtle visual motif and a simple judge-facing explanation.

The name is short, direct, and consistent with the product's job of binding business context, authority, and execution into a coherent workflow. Do not delay the hackathon build by reopening naming unless an actual legal, domain, or product-confusion issue is found.

| Name / descriptor | Role | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| **Bind** | Main product and brand | Use this consistently. |

| **Bind Workflows** | Feature name | Use only if the information architecture genuinely needs a workflow area name. |
| **Bind OS** | Broad future positioning | Do not use for this slice. It overstates the current product breadth. |

### Tagline options

1. **Your business runs on your rules.** Recommended. It is simple, memorable, and communicates bounded autonomy.
2. More routine work handled. Control stays yours.
3. Agents for the way business actually works.
4. Bring agents into the business. Keep the decisions yours.
5. Real work, coordinated. Business authority stays put.

Use option 1 as the hero headline companion/tagline. Use option 3 as an explanatory supporting line. Do not rotate through multiple taglines within the same page.

## C. Recommended Brand Direction

### Creative concept: Calm control, connected work

The visual experience should make Bind feel like a premium operations product built around actual people and actual work. The site can be editorial and cinematic; the app should be more direct and task-oriented. Both need to share the same typography, surface colors, interaction language, icon family, and state semantics.

### Art direction

- Use natural-light photography of real operators in kitchens, stockrooms, cafés, or service environments. Favor candid, composed human moments over staged “business team smiling at laptop” images.
- Use crop and contrast deliberately. One strong image per major story section is better than a gallery of generic stock photography.
- Keep main page sections mostly warm white or paper. Reserve deep ink/teal for the hero, footer, nav, and a small number of high-confidence emphasis surfaces.
- Use warm orange only for key actions and limited visual emphasis. It should be recognizable as the action color.
- Use product previews with real labels and workflow facts. A visible panel should resemble the app a judge can actually open, not a fictional dashboard.
- Prefer thin borders, firm alignment, and calm spacing over soft gradients, excessive floating cards, or glassmorphism.

### What to borrow from the provided visual reference

- Strong first-screen image and short headline.
- Editorial section rhythm with meaningful changes of scale.
- Wide whitespace and clean contrast.
- Product preview cards integrated into the narrative.
- Clear CTA repeated at the beginning and end of the page.
- Human, tactile atmosphere instead of a purely technical landing page.

### What not to borrow

- Its wordmark or distinct visual identity.
- Its health-oriented content, stats, membership card, testimonials, or category list.
- Exact section compositions, image crops, type treatment, or copy.
- Any claim that Bind has users, benchmarks, partners, or outcomes not supported by evidence.

### Visual motifs

**Primary motif:** three strokes or three softly curved bands that meet at a single joint and continue as one path. This is an abstract expression of Context + Authority + Fulfilment.

**Secondary motif:** a line that passes through three checkpoints. Use it sparingly in the workflow timeline, pitch deck, and diagrams.

**Avoid:** literal chains, padlocks as the whole brand idea, coins, glowing circuitry, brains, orbiting nodes, AI sparkles everywhere, random network graphs, or futuristic grids.

## D. Logo Direction

### Recommended logo system

1. **Wordmark:** `bind` in lowercase, with a stable geometric sans-serif. Use the proper product name “Bind” in prose and headings; the lowercase treatment is a visual wordmark decision.
2. **Symbol:** a compact “join” glyph based on three clean strokes or ribbons converging into one connector. The symbol should be flat, balanced, and legible at 16–24px.
3. **Lockup:** symbol + wordmark in horizontal form for site header and app shell. Symbol-only version may be used as a favicon if it remains distinct at small size.
4. **Reverse version:** white wordmark and symbol on deep ink/teal.

### Symbol geometry

- Use one coherent stroke weight and rounded terminals only if it supports the typography.
- Avoid fine overlaps that disappear at small sizes.
- The mark should read as connection/coordination, not as an explicit chain or a blockchain logo.
- The three joins can imply three parts, but the logo must not need an explanation to remain visually simple.
- Do not make the mascot, a literal knot illustration, or a 3D object the main logo.

### Clear-space and usage

- Minimum clear space: approximately the height of the symbol around the lockup.
- Do not stretch, outline, add glow, put a gradient inside the glyph, or change the orange/teal balance arbitrarily.
- Use orange sparingly in the mark. Default logo versions should be ink on paper and white on deep teal.
- For the hackathon slice, a well-typeset wordmark plus the simple join glyph is better than spending days on a bespoke logo system.

## E. Color + Typography System

Use the design tokens in `DESIGN.md` as the canonical implementation values. The following explains their brand intent.

### Color roles

| Color | Existing token | Brand job |
|---|---|---|
| Deep ink `#17211F` | `--bind-ink` | Trust, strong text, app structure, dark ending section. |
| Deep teal `#174A4A` | `--bind-teal` | Brand recognition, hero overlays, authority/coordination emphasis. |
| Warm paper `#FAF8F4` | `--bind-paper` | Editorial background; warmth without looking beige-heavy. |
| White `#FFFFFF` | `--bind-white` | Product panels, forms, clean work surfaces. |
| Teal mist `#E5EFEB` | `--bind-teal-pale` | Context panels, calm grouped information. |
| Warm orange `#F45B36` | `--bind-orange` | Primary CTA and a restrained active signal. |
| Orange mist `#FCEAE3` | `--bind-orange-pale` | Secondary action context or lightweight callout. |
| Border `#E6E4DD` | `--bind-border` | Quiet separation and structure. |

Use semantic success, warning, and danger colors only on explicit states. Do not tint large areas with status colors. All status labels require text, not color alone.

### Typography

- **Headlines:** Manrope, medium to semibold; use short lines and meaningful breaks.
- **UI/body:** Inter, regular to medium; legible at small sizes and for technical evidence.
- **Data/hash fields:** use the current system monospace stack, only for values that benefit from it.
- **Use:** generous headline scale on the homepage, restrained app headings, strong labels, short explanatory paragraphs.
- **Avoid:** all-thin typography, extremely tight body line-height, decorative display fonts, full-uppercase paragraphs, or using different typefaces for every section.

### Data and status visualization

- Keep charts incidental. Do not introduce fabricated “time saved,” “efficiency,” spend, adoption, or performance graphs.
- Use a step timeline for workflow progression and a compact policy decision block for business rules.
- Show evidence in readable label/value rows with copy controls for long hashes.
- Use a clear icon plus label and plain-language description for policy decisions and lifecycle statuses.

## F. Mascot / Character System

### Strong recommendation

**Do not use a cartoon robot or a personality-led mascot in the operational UI.** Bind's credibility rests on business control and accountability. A conventional cute AI assistant would pull the brand toward generic AI software and could imply that the agent itself is the authority.

Use a restrained abstract character system as a secondary illustration asset. Working art-direction name: **“The Join.”** This is an internal production label, not necessarily a public character name.

### Character concept

A small, soft-edged form assembled from three ribbon-like segments that meet at one clear joint. It should feel tactile and deliberate, like a shaped paper or enamel object, not a glowing digital creature. No robot body, circuitry, face, eyes, floating hands, or coin/chain parts.

### Visual language

- Three rounded segments with consistent thickness.
- Deep teal and paper as the base; orange marks the joining point or one small active segment.
- Mostly flat 2D or subtle paper-like depth. No chrome, neon rim light, heavy 3D shine, or multicolor gradients.
- Simple silhouette that can be reduced to a monochrome stamp.

### States / poses

| Situation | Visual treatment | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Empty / ready | Three segments sit near each other with a small visible opening | Set up the workflow or connect the missing piece. |
| Working | Segments form a clear connected path | Workflow is being processed; pair with actual textual progress. |
| Needs owner | One segment pauses just before the join, orange used sparingly | Decision is required from the owner. Never imply approval happened. |
| Blocked | Segments remain visibly separate | A rule or required input prevented continuation. Explain why in text. |
| Reconciled | The three segments meet cleanly as one form | May accompany confirmed completion only after the three-way match passes. |

Do not animate these poses as a substitute for backend state. An animation may transition only after the application receives a real state result.

### Where it can appear

- First-run welcome and empty states.
- A small brand illustration in the About/How Bind Works section.
- Pitch-deck dividers and social media explainers.
- Onboarding illustration where a real form or workflow needs a warmer introduction.

### Where it should not appear

- As a floating chatbot that follows the user.
- Beside every status label or action button.
- Inside transaction rows where it could distract from evidence.
- As the primary logo.
- In any state where character expression could imply that a vendor is live, a payment succeeded, or goods were delivered.

## G. Product UX Flow

### Primary user journey

1. The visitor understands Bind's promise from the landing page.
2. The visitor selects **Open app** and reaches `/app`.
3. The app opens a labelled demo workspace if the live user has not configured a business.
4. The operator sees the current kitchen replenishment situation and the next valid action.
5. The operator starts or opens the low-stock workflow.
6. Bind shows the stock trigger, request/source attribution, and deterministic policy result.
7. If the action exceeds authority, the workflow is held for an owner decision. If the action is permitted, the implemented settlement path can run under its configured policy.
8. If a transaction is confirmed on Arc Testnet, the record displays that evidence and the correct network.
9. Physical fulfilment remains pending until the business's three-way match criteria are satisfied.
10. The user opens the evidence drawer to inspect the decision hash, policy checks, contract, transaction, source adapter, and fulfilment state.

### First-run approach

For the hackathon slice, open a prepared `Demo workspace` with a clear label and useful example records. Do not force a lengthy onboarding wizard before a judge can see the workflow. A small setup summary can show which context is configured and what is still demo/fixture data.

Longer-term, onboarding can become a guided business setup that captures products, suppliers, budget, operating hours, approval thresholds, and communication preferences. Do not expose editable fields that are not connected to backend validation and persisted state.

### Required core screens

1. `/` — brand story, product value, workflow example, trust/evidence, Open app CTA.
2. `/app` — operations overview with current workflow and the next valid action.
3. `/app/workflows/:workflowId` — workflow timeline, policy outcome, source attribution, settlement state, fulfilment state.
4. Evidence drawer or evidence section — hashes, contract details, transaction link, policy and source evidence.
5. Approval state, if present — owner can understand what is being approved, amount/counterparty, which rule triggered review, and whether the approval action has actually been committed.

Do not add fake settings, agent marketplace, agent directory, analytics suite, or integrations catalog to make the app seem bigger.

### App information hierarchy

1. Current situation and next step.
2. Workflow item / requested action.
3. Authority decision: allowed, held, or denied, with the reason.
4. Timeline and latest source/evidence.
5. Technical details, collapsed until opened.

### UX microcopy

Use factual labels and direct verbs.

| Scenario | Preferred copy | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| CTA | `Open app` | `Launch the future`, `Start magic` |
| Workflow trigger | `Run low-stock check` | `Deploy AI` |
| Approval required | `Owner approval required` | `Almost complete` |
| Simulated sourcing | `Supplier source: simulated adapter` | `Supplier connected` |
| Confirmed testnet payment | `Settled on Arc Testnet` | `Supplier paid` unless the source proves it means the actual supplier payment |
| Waiting on goods | `Awaiting fulfilment match` | `Complete` |
| Blocked by rule | `Blocked: vendor is not approved` | `Something went wrong` |
| Missing evidence | `No transaction recorded` | Empty values or fake hashes |
| Demo data | `Demo workspace` | Unlabelled seeded records |

### Empty, loading, error, and success states

- **Empty:** explain which piece of business context is missing and the next step to add it.
- **Loading:** show the specific action in progress. Do not fake a percent-complete indicator for an operation with unknown duration.
- **Needs approval:** explain the exact reason and decision needed; keep the action explicit.
- **Blocked:** show the failing rule and, where safe, how to fix it.
- **Error:** preserve prior evidence and allow a safe retry only when idempotency/duplicate behavior is addressed.
- **Settled, not fulfilled:** show payment state and physical fulfilment state as separate rows; neither overrides the other.
- **Completed:** use only when the backend's three-way match says it is complete.

## H. Landing Page Direction

### Core objective

In the first screen, visitors should understand that Bind helps a business coordinate repeatable work with AI agents while keeping business rules and authority in the owner's hands.

### Page structure

1. **Header:** Bind wordmark, two or three section links, strong `Open app` action.
2. **Hero:** human business photography, confident headline, one supporting sentence, `Open app`, optional `See how it works` link, and one real or honestly labelled workflow preview.
3. **The threefold Bind:** Context, Authority, Fulfilment, explained simply.
4. **Workflow story:** low stock becomes a policy-checked replenishment request, then a traceable decision.
5. **Product preview:** a larger crop of the actual operations workspace, with source attribution and lifecycle states.
6. **Authority and evidence:** explain how approval boundaries and decision records work without promising absolute security.
7. **Closing CTA:** `Keep the business in control.` with `Open app`.
8. **Footer:** concise product statement, route/repository links that actually exist, no invented policies or partner endorsements.

### Recommended hero copy

Eyebrow: `BUSINESS OPERATIONS, WITH YOU IN CONTROL`

Headline: **Your business runs on your rules.**

Body: `Bind coordinates agents around the way your business works, with clear limits and a record of every important action.`

Primary CTA: `Open app`

Secondary CTA: `See how it works`

Alternate headline if the product descriptor needs to be more explicit: **Bring agents into the work. Keep control of the business.**

### Hero composition

- A wide, warm-toned photograph with the main subject positioned away from the copy column.
- Dark ink/teal overlay behind copy only as strong as needed for contrast.
- Text left-aligned on desktop; narrow and stacked on mobile.
- A single small evidence or workflow card at the image edge, not a pile of floating widgets.
- Header visible immediately; no intro movie, logo animation, or scroll lock.

### Landing-page craft rules

- Make section layouts vary for a reason: hero image, three-part concept, split workflow preview, evidence panel, closing CTA.
- Keep each section to one idea.
- Avoid a row of six feature cards, fake KPI counters, testimonial carousels, noisy pill clusters, and empty gradients.
- Use actual Bind app screenshots when available. Before that, use fixture-backed mock UI and visibly label it as a demo.
- The orange CTA appears consistently; all secondary links look secondary.
- The full page should read as one narrative, not a concatenation of templates.

## I. Pitch Deck Visual Direction

### Story to tell

Businesses do not need to become agent-native to benefit from agents. Agents need to fit into business context, authority, and real-world work. Bind is the coordination layer that makes that fit concrete.

### Suggested seven-slide deck

**Slide 1 — Bind**  
Headline: `Your business runs on your rules.`  
Visual: human operator in context with the three-part join motif. Keep logo and headline dominant, with only one concise description.

**Slide 2 — The operational problem**  
Headline: `Routine coordination eats the day.`  
Visual: simple operating path with stock, supplier, budget, approval, payment, delivery. Highlight the handoffs and context loss without inventing quantified pain.

**Slide 3 — What Bind does**  
Headline: `One workflow. Shared context. Bounded authority.`  
Visual: three connected blocks: Context → Authority → Fulfilment. Use clear labels and one line each.

**Slide 4 — Show, do not describe**  
Headline: `A low-stock signal becomes a governed decision.`  
Visual: screenshot of `/app` with a clear step path and one highlighted action. The screen should be readable, not shrunk to illegibility.

**Slide 5 — Authority belongs to the business**  
Headline: `Agents can act only within the rules.`  
Visual: deterministic policy checks, allowed/held outcomes, and owner approval. Show a blocked path if it exists, not a staged false success.

**Slide 6 — Settlement is not fulfilment**  
Headline: `A transaction is evidence of payment, not proof of delivery.`  
Visual: Arc Testnet transaction evidence on one side, `Awaiting three-way match` on the other. This is Bind's strongest technical and product-truth moment.

**Slide 7 — What this unlocks**  
Headline: `Start with one real workflow.`  
Visual: kitchen supplies workflow today, restrained diagram of how the same coordination pattern can extend to other business workflows later. Label future directions as future, not current features.

### Deck design rules

- One claim per slide; six to twelve words for the core headline when practical.
- Use a large title, one proof visual, and a short explanation rather than walls of text.
- Use real screenshots, test evidence, and correctly labelled Arcscan artifacts.
- Avoid fabricated metrics, invented customer logos, and implying Tameion/Circle endorsement beyond the program facts.
- Use the join motif as a connective element, never a decoration on every corner.

### Recommended demo narrative

1. Start at the marketing page and state the problem in plain terms.
2. Click `Open app` and show the kitchen workflow.
3. Run or open the low-stock trigger.
4. Explain the deterministic vendor/budget authority result.
5. Show the approval or permitted execution path.
6. Open the recorded Arc Testnet transaction only if the transaction is present and confirmed.
7. Return to the workflow and emphasize that goods remain unfulfilled until the three-way match passes.
8. End on the principle: Bind connects context, authority, and real-world fulfilment, without pretending one is a substitute for another.

### Final “wow” moment

**The wow moment is the honest contradiction other demos often hide:** the payment can be settled on Arc Testnet while the goods are still not confirmed received. Bind shows both states together, with the decision hash and transaction evidence. This proves that the system understands workflow reality, not only transaction submission.

## J. Image Generation, Figma, and Implementation Prompts

Use these prompts as inputs to the appropriate design tool. They are art-direction briefs, not permission to invent product evidence. Generated images must not contain UI text, logos, transaction hashes, brand names, or fake product screenshots unless the purpose is explicitly just a rough non-production mockup.

### 1. Hero photography prompt

> Create a premium editorial photograph for a modern business operations product. Show a real small-business operator in a working kitchen or stockroom, naturally checking supplies before service. Warm natural window light, tactile stainless steel and honest work surfaces, composed but not staged, human expression focused and capable, cinematic wide crop with generous uncluttered negative space on the left for dark overlay and headline. Color grade: restrained deep teal shadows, warm paper highlights, small natural orange details only if present in the environment. The image should feel candid, credible, high-end, and human. No robots, no holograms, no glowing interfaces, no circuit graphics, no cryptocurrency imagery, no logos, no readable text, no fake dashboards, no exaggerated smiles. Wide 16:9 composition.

### 2. Secondary workflow photograph prompt

> Create a realistic premium editorial photograph of a small food-business operator checking a delivery crate and ingredient stock after a supplier drop-off. Natural light, tactile material detail, candid posture, practical environment, uncluttered framing, warm whites with subtle deep teal shadows, restrained documentary photography, believable proportions and real operational context. Leave a clean area for interface overlay if needed. No text, logos, robots, glowing UI, coin imagery, or artificial futuristic styling.

### 3. Abstract “Join” character prompt

> Design a minimal abstract brand character for a premium business-operations product. Construct the form from exactly three soft, rounded ribbon segments that join at one clear connector point. The form should evoke connection, coordination, and a governed path, not a chain link. Flat 2D vector-inspired rendering with warm paper, deep ink/teal, and one restrained warm-orange join point. Strong silhouette, tactile simplicity, low detail, no eyes, no face, no limbs, no robot anatomy, no circuitry, no gradients that glow, no chrome, no coin or blockchain symbolism. Show five isolated poses on a plain warm-white background: ready/open, connected/working, paused for approval, separated/blocked, and fully joined/reconciled. ### 4. Brand mark exploration prompt

> Explore a minimalist identity for “bind”, a business-side agent coordination product. Create a lowercase wordmark and a separate compact symbol built from three consistent strokes joining into one path. Premium, precise, grounded, warm, and quiet. ### 5. Figma / UX design prompt

> Design Bind as a cohesive responsive web product with two related modes: an editorial marketing site and a practical operations app. Use Manrope headings, Inter UI text, warm-white paper backgrounds, deep ink/teal structure, orange only for primary actions, quiet neutral borders, restrained rounded corners, and Heroicons for interface icons. > Build a seven-slide pitch deck for Bind using a premium editorial style: warm paper, deep ink/teal, restrained orange, large confident headlines, real small-business photography, and legible product screenshots. ## K. Final Creative Recommendation

Build **Bind** as a warm, premium, editorial brand around one sharp proposition: **Your business runs on your rules.** Use the supplied screenshot for high-level pacing, a human hero, spacious storytelling, and confident calls to action. Make the product feel distinct through its threefold model and strong evidence design.

Keep the brand system restrained:

- One wordmark and one simple join glyph.
- One primary type pair: Manrope + Inter.
- One warm neutral base, one deep teal/ink family, one action orange.
- Heroicons for actual interface actions and statuses.
- Human business photography instead of tech stock imagery.
- A secondary abstract character only where it improves warmth, not as a substitute for product clarity.
- A functional `/app` destination connected to the implemented workflow.
- An evidence-first demo showing business policy, Arc Testnet settlement, and physical fulfilment as separate concepts.

Do not expand scope to feel like a bigger company. Make one workflow look trustworthy, work correctly, and tell the story clearly. A coherent small product will be more memorable than an overbuilt dashboard with a louder visual identity.
